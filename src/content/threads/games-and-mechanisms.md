title: Game theory and mechanism design
pillar: network-control

summary: Satisfaction games and market mechanisms for sharing a network among users who do not cooperate.
---

Classical resource allocation assumes every user maximizes its own utility, and the outcome is a Nash equilibrium. A user rarely needs the maximum. It needs enough. Games in satisfaction form replace maximization with satisfaction constraints. We introduced efficient satisfaction equilibria for power control, designed distributed dynamics that provably reach them, extended the framework to fragile common-pool resources and to risk-aware players, and showed consistent gains over Nash outcomes.

The same lens applies to infrastructure sharing. Network slicing builds customized logical networks on shared infrastructure, where tenants with private valuations compete for the same capacity. We designed a market mechanism for slicing and characterized the competitive equilibria it induces and the decentralized dynamics that reach them.
