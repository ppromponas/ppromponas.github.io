title: Quantum switches

summary: Memory, scheduling and rate-fidelity tradeoffs in entanglement switches.
---

A quantum switch receives requests for end-to-end entanglement while probabilistic link-level generation produces Bell pairs with its neighbors. Those pairs sit in scarce, noisy memories and must be consumed by swaps before decoherence destroys them. A flexible switch is reconfigurable, with optical switching in front of its memories and sources, so incoming and outgoing entanglement can be redirected and memories and sources reallocated across links as demand shifts.

This thread builds analytical models of that process and control policies for it: throughput-optimal policies for reallocating memories and sources under fidelity constraints, what the throughput and fidelity regions of a switch look like, and how all-photonic and memory-equipped architectures trade rate against fidelity.
