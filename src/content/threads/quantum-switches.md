title: Quantum switches
pillar: quantum-networks

summary: Memory, scheduling and rate-fidelity tradeoffs in entanglement switches.
---

A quantum switch receives requests for end-to-end entanglement while probabilistic link-level generation produces Bell pairs with its neighbors. Those pairs sit in scarce, noisy memories and must be consumed by swaps before decoherence destroys them. In a flexible switch the same memories also generate the pairs, so reassigning memory from one link to another changes where entanglement is produced.

This thread builds analytical models of that process and control policies for it: how to allocate limited memory to maximize entanglement rates under fidelity constraints, what the throughput and fidelity regions of a switch look like, and how all-photonic and memory-equipped architectures trade rate against fidelity.
